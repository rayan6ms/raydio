# Playback and resource bug audit — 2026-10-03

Seven actionable defects were found in Raydio and its pinned Crust/Mantle paths.
Eight focused characterization tests establish the problematic behavior or its
constituent state transitions. Two findings can prematurely replace a playing
song; the others concern playlist filtering, cancellation, preparation cleanup,
admission and queue bounds.

This report records the audited baseline. All seven repairs and corrected
regression coverage are documented in [the repair report](BUG-FIXES-2026-10-03.md).
The characterization tests intentionally assert the baseline's
bad behavior, so passing them confirms the reproductions rather than correctness.
All temporary test additions were removed byte-for-byte after retaining their
patches and output. Production, Discord playback, credentials and networking
were not changed. No new live or six-hour measurement was performed.

## B1 — A wall-clock end watchdog can replace a still-playing song

Raydio: `src/session.rs:707`, `:1094`, `:1272`.

`schedule_end` arms a deadline from the declared track duration, current position
and 15-second grace. Later `playerUpdate` messages update the media position but
do not reconsider that deadline. On expiry, `tick` unconditionally calls
`queue.finish()` and starts the next track, without consulting current media
progress or confirming EOF.

**Trigger:** media playback falls more than the grace behind the original
wall-clock schedule, for example after accumulated sender scheduling delays or
a recoverable voice interruption. Incorrect short duration metadata can have
the same effect. Explicit user pause already disarms the deadline and is not the
path being reported here.

**Reproduction:** expire the watchdog for a declared 20-second current track,
then deliver a fresh connected `playerUpdate` at 5,000 ms. Its deadline remains
expired. The next actual `tick` advances to the next track and records a watchdog
event, despite the newly reported position being well before the declared end.
The fixture sets the expired state directly; it does not reproduce a live
network interruption or measure how frequently Oracle reaches that state.

**Impact:** audible truncation and false natural-finish/history/loop semantics.
This could account for an interrupted song when the specific watchdog event
exists in its logs; the audit does not establish it as the cause of previously
reported THE CLUB failures or receiver packet loss.

**Fix:** separate media-progress recovery from missing-end-event recovery.
Track validated progress and generation, reconsider the remaining budget on
fresh updates, and verify terminal/backend state before treating an expiry as
natural EOF. Do not keep extending a stalled track forever merely because
periodic updates arrive; repeated unchanged position must have a bounded
recovery policy. Intentional pause, reconnect and EOF drain need distinct states.

**Acceptance:** advancing media after a delay must continue; unchanged media
must eventually recover/fail with the correct reason; a lost end event must
advance exactly once. Include stale updates and repeated encoded tracks.

## B2 — Missing playlist duration can cut a healthy finite track after 15 seconds

Mantle: `crates/mantle-media/src/youtube.rs:4678`.
Raydio: `src/resolver.rs:123`, `src/session.rs:707`, `src/playback.rs:41`.

When a playlist renderer lacks a string `lengthSeconds`, Mantle emits a finite
track with `Duration::ZERO`. Raydio accepts that track: its admission checks an
upper duration bound but not whether the duration is known. It then schedules
the same end watchdog at `0 - position + 15,000 ms` (saturating arithmetic).
Advance preparation also considers the unknown-duration current song to be
within its 45-second lead immediately.

**Reproductions:** a playable playlist renderer without `lengthSeconds` becomes
a non-stream track with duration zero. A session given such a track arms its end
deadline in **14,999 ms**, rather than waiting for genuine media completion.
B1's actual expired-tick reproduction establishes the replacement path.
No production playlist item with missing duration was captured in this audit.

**Impact:** a perfectly playable longer song can be truncated or restarted every
15 seconds. Metadata absence becomes an audio-control instruction. Preparation
can also hold the successor's storage/connection for much longer than intended.

**Fix:** preserve unknown duration as unknown throughout admission, end recovery
and preparation. Do not reinterpret it as a known zero-length track or as a live
stream. Either hydrate reliable duration under a bounded lookup or allow finite
unknown-duration playback with a progress/stall policy and genuine EOF events.
Until duration is reliable, do not infer that it is close to its end.

**Acceptance:** an unknown-duration finite song lasting more than 15 seconds must
not be cut or immediately trigger advance preparation; known durations and real
streams must retain their respective policies. Cover numeric/missing/malformed
duration fields and discovery failure without silently inventing a duration.

## B3 — The playlist parser accepts `isPlayable: false`

Mantle: `crates/mantle-media/src/youtube.rs:4666`.
Raydio consequence: `src/playback.rs:91`.

The parser rejects absent/null `isPlayable`, but not the boolean value `false`.
If the other renderer fields are present, it produces a normal playable track.

**Reproduction:** change an otherwise valid fixture renderer's `isPlayable` to
false. `parse_playlist_tracks` still returns **one finite track**.

**Impact:** known unavailable entries consume queue capacity, source requests
and startup time. If they fail during playback, they count towards the bot's
three-consecutive-failures policy. Three adjacent entries can therefore clear
the rest of an otherwise useful playlist. That consequence follows the queue
policy; no live playlist with three such entries was fetched in this audit.

**Fix:** require a valid affirmative playability flag and skip explicitly
unplayable entries. Keep omission counts meaningful where the source API can
represent them. An unexpected flag type should not become affirmative.

**Acceptance:** false, null, absent and invalid flags cannot enqueue tracks;
valid entries surrounding them retain their order. Verify that filtering known
unplayable entries cannot trigger playback's failure-streak cutoff.

## B4 — Encoded playback does network loading without load/source permits

Crust: `crates/crust-server/src/player.rs:106`, `:123`, `:1129`, `:1808`.
Actual network path: `crates/crust-mantle-adapter/src/lib.rs:1440`, `:1492`, `:1920`.
Raydio limits: `src/backend.rs:78`.

The identifier path and new preparation path acquire load, source and outbound
permits. The encoded path acquires only an outbound permit, and even that is
reused for an existing active player. Decoding the descriptor itself is local,
but subsequent `mantle.play` invokes `open_playback`, including YouTube discovery
and opening/downloading media unless a prepared/completed cache matches.

**Reproduction:** configure one load permit, one source permit and three outbound
permits. Hold an ordinary load open so load/source admission is occupied. A second
load correctly returns **HTTP 503**, while an encoded PATCH on another player
starts a track with **HTTP 200**. The fake adapter isolates admission behavior;
the production adapter's additional network work is established by call tracing.

**Impact:** Raydio's configured four-load/eight-source bounds do not protect its
normal encoded cold-start path. Concurrent guild requests can exceed those
budgets, compete with active playback/preparation, and consume additional workers,
HTTP connections and buffers. Global player/outbound limits and the progressive
downloader's own limit still exist; this is not an unlimited-allocation claim.

**Fix:** put source-opening admission at the layer that knows whether Play needs
network work. A real cold encoded open must acquire load/source capacity, while
prepared adoption and completed-cache replay should remain cheap and offline.
Avoid nested acquisition/deadlock when identifier loading already owns permits;
preserve the connection permit while an actual downloader still uses it.

**Acceptance:** concurrent cold encoded opens obey the same global ceiling as
search and preparation. Cancellation/errors return permits; ready adoption and
completed repeats work without unnecessary source admission; replacement cannot
temporarily escape the intended connection bound.

## B5 — A completed preparation result can resurrect state after player cleanup

Raydio: `src/session.rs:608`, `:684`, `:1301`.

Cleanup calls `preparations.abort_all()` and clears the remembered target before
destroying the backend player. `abort_all` does not remove already completed
JoinSet results. The successful-result arm later accepts every epoch, restoring
the old target even though cleanup invalidated and destroyed that player.

**Reproduction:** complete an old-epoch successful preparation acknowledgement,
then run actual cleanup. Its successful result remains available afterwards.
Applying the exact success assignment from `run` restores `old-next-track`.
Two subsequent actual preparation attempts send null cancellation to the
destroyed player and fail; the remembered old target remains. The harness tests
the state components and assignment rather than running the entire Discord
gateway loop, and the original preparation success is injected, not obtained
from the fake media adapter.

**Impact:** the idle session can repeatedly request cancellation against a
nonexistent player, retrying every five seconds and emitting unnecessary warning
logs. A later session can temporarily inherit an old remembered intent instead
of an accurate backend preparation state.

**Fix:** abort and drain preparation completions as part of the player-destroying
cleanup boundary, or associate remembered acknowledgements with the backend
player's lifetime. Preserve the intentional ordering rule for a Stop that keeps
the same player alive: blindly ignoring all old-epoch successes would reintroduce
the older accepted-intent cancellation race.

**Acceptance:** already-completed and in-flight acknowledgements cannot revive
state after Leave/disconnect/idle cleanup; no idle 404 retry loop remains.
Stop with an overtaken accepted intent must still submit cancellation in order.
Rejoin with the same encoded successor must prepare it on the new player.

## B6 — A cancelled authentication waiter remains blocked behind another request

Mantle: `crates/mantle-media/src/youtube.rs:2228`, `:2303`, `:2382`.
Related inspected path: `:2416` (player-script cache).

OAuth access is serialized with a blocking mutex. Its owner can retain that
mutex through a remote token refresh. Another caller performs its initial
cancellation check, then waits on `.lock()` without any cancellable wait or
independent deadline. Cancelling that caller cannot wake it. The public refresh
path also lacks a recheck immediately after acquiring the lock.

**Reproduction:** hold the real manager's OAuth mutex, arrange for the waiting
call's initial cancellation check to pass, then cancel it. It still has not
returned after a **150 ms** hold. Once the lock is released it returns
`InvalidAuthentication` for the unauthenticated fixture, rather than cancellation.
The initial-check callback removes the race about when cancellation occurs.
The fixture simulates ownership contention without making a token request.

**Impact:** cancelled source work can retain a worker/admission resources until
some other request completes. A slow refresh can delay unrelated guild source
work and cleanup. The production Companion path need not use OAuth on every
successful request; this primarily affects authentication/native-client fallback
and explicit refresh. The player-script cache holds a similar mutex across
network acquisition, but that related path was inspected rather than separately
reproduced and is not counted as an eighth defect.

**Fix:** retain single-owner refresh/cache population without uncancellable
network-duration lock waits. Use bounded, cancellable single-flight waiting and
keep short shared-state locks separate from HTTP work. Check cancellation after
waiting and before returning cached data. Do not introduce duplicate refreshes,
unbounded waiter lists or log secret values.

**Acceptance:** a cancelled waiter returns promptly while the refresh owner is
still pending. Remaining waiters share one bounded refresh; failure/shutdown
release all waiters and permits. Verify the player-script acquisition equivalent.

## B7 — Previous bypasses the advertised queue limit

Raydio: `src/playback.rs:112`; admission: `:57`.

After the queue advances and is refilled to its limit, `previous` takes a track
out of history and inserts the current one into upcoming. Except when queue-loop
deduplication happens to remove a matching entry, this increases the counted queue
length without checking its maximum.

**Reproduction:** queue cap **2**, play the first entry into history, enqueue one
replacement to refill the queue, then Previous. Counted queue length becomes **3**.
The operation is reachable through the normal control path.

**Impact:** the queue's resource/admission contract is inconsistent. It remains
bounded by the ordinary queue plus the bounded history; this is not unbounded
growth or a demonstrated memory problem on Oracle.

**Fix:** make previous-history restoration part of the queue admission policy.
At capacity, reject the operation without mutating history/current/upcoming, or
use an explicit documented reservation policy that preserves requested tracks.
Do not silently discard an unrelated queued song to manufacture capacity.

**Acceptance:** Off/Track/Queue loop modes, a full queue and repeated Previous
never exceed the chosen bound; refusal is transactional and history remains valid.

## Sender, media and diagnostic review limits

The pinned Oto sender was inspected for readiness/wake ordering, retained frames
during pause/DAVE transitions, absolute deadlines, same-packet UDP retry and nonce
ownership, and terminal-state propagation into Crust. The bridge/source terminal
path, media read-ahead/cancellation, staged-cache reopening, progressive publication
and preparation permit ownership were also traced. No additional confirmed Oto
wire-format, encryption or packet-loss defect was established in this pass.
That is not a claim of exhaustive protocol correctness.

The inspected preparation design still has important diagnostic distinctions:
HTTP 202 acknowledges an intent rather than readiness; failed speculative opening
falls back to normal Play; preparation INFO targets are not enabled by the current
default Raydio log filter. These are observability/qualification limits, not three
additional proven playback bugs. Preserve separate requested/ready/consumed/failed
observations when validating repairs, including per-guild correlation without
logging encoded descriptors or signed URLs.

Sender unavailable-frame counters can include intentional terminal/pause silence.
Likewise, decoded quiet across song boundaries contains recorded silence. Neither
should alone be labelled packet loss or a source interruption. A future live check
should correlate track generation/position, sender deadlines and UDP outcomes,
source read/cache progress, receiver loss/discard/concealment, and decoded PCM.
External receiver outages must remain separate from bot failures.

## Recommended repair and qualification order

1. Fix B1/B2 together, retaining distinct progress-stall and missing-EOF recovery.
2. Correct B3 and qualify mixed playable/unplayable and unknown-duration playlists.
3. Fix B5 while keeping same-player Stop ordering intact.
4. Implement B4/B6 together with bounded concurrency/cancellation qualification.
5. Enforce B7 without losing history or requested tracks.
6. Turn these characterization cases into assertions of the intended behavior,
   run the affected repository checks, and only then deploy a candidate for a
   short uninterrupted receiver test followed by endurance qualification.

This audit supports targeted repairs. It does not establish that any repair
will reduce downstream net packet loss, and it does not justify changing the
codec, gain, bitrate, progressive prefix or pacing merely to improve counters.
